# Evidence-Grounded Manuscripts

For a new paper, record the question, literature-supported gap, proposed contribution, article type/language, available materials, and unresolved issues in `notes/research-brief.md` or an existing equivalent. Maintain a claim-to-evidence note when multiple sources need reconciliation.

Select an outline appropriate to the work. Numerical-method papers may emphasize formulation, consistency/stability, implementation, benchmarks, and limitations; theoretical and experimental papers need a different balance. Write evidence-supported sections first, then align the introduction, abstract, contributions, and conclusions with the completed body.

Distinguish derivations, observations, interpretations, and hypotheses. Use explicit assumptions and scope limits. Avoid unsupported novelty, superiority, first-of-its-kind, or generalization claims. Never invent numerical results, bibliography records, authors, funding, approvals, or declarations. Use visible `[TODO: ...]` text for missing evidence outside citation syntax.

Preserve the user's argument, notation, terminology, and scientific meaning when editing. Define symbols, units, metrics, experimental conditions, baselines, and the limits of comparisons. Check that every claim in the abstract/conclusion has support in the body and that each cited source supports its associated statement.

Keep original research figures/data, source notes, and project-specific reproducibility scripts with the manuscript. Reuse verified outputs during ordinary rendering; perform research recomputation when requested or needed to establish the claimed evidence. Synthetic examples must be labeled as such and separated from real findings and author information.
